# Responsibility, Consequences and Slogans

**21 October 2026, biological-origin follow-up.** Context only. Canon: [world and campaign](world-and-campaign.md). Screen timing: [story and trailer](story-context.md). Scientific limits: [science notes](science-notes.md).

## 1. The owner's intended message

Humanity kept treating the symptoms of its behavior while postponing the changes that would reduce the damage. Waste-eating creatures were a useful invention, but society treated their success as permission to keep producing and discarding more. The later atmospheric treatment became another promise that the consequences could be managed without changing those habits. Its unexpected interaction with the creatures turned that dependence into catastrophe.

The requested “humanity got what it deserved” feeling is expressed as **consequences finally reaching the people who kept postponing responsibility**. The narrator can be severe and regretful. The creatures themselves are biological threats, not judges sent to punish humanity. The catastrophe is not proof that every individual victim deserved death; survivors include people who warned, repaired and tried to change things.

Scientific effort and cleanup still matter. The failure is dependence on interventions without enough prevention, oversight and follow-through. The ending earns hope by showing sustained work: recover resources, contain sources, reduce waste and protect what remains. HOPE must embody a different habit, not advertise another effortless cure.

## 2. Selected draft tagline

> We kept throwing it away. Until it came back for us.

Use this as the main thematic line in promotional copy or an optional dedicated story card. It connects discarded material, avoidance and the return of danger. It is a metaphor: biological creatures occupy the scrap; metal did not become alive.

Do not squeeze this full line beneath the existing four-second trailer title. The storyboard uses a shorter opening line; a longer tagline card requires an intentional retiming and readability review.

| “We did not run out of warnings. We ran out of time.” | Regret after collapse | Optional longer narrative copy; do not add an extra timed beat without revising the edit |

These are original drafts, not mandatory text to show all at once. The main story must still explain the two separate inventions, biological mutation, refuge and cadet mission. A slogan cannot replace that causal chain.

## 4. Optional longer synopsis passage

We made creatures to consume the waste we would not stop creating. They worked on Earth and in the stations above it, turning yesterday's refuse into tomorrow's resources. Their success bought us time. We spent it making more of the same mistakes.

Then came a treatment for the sky. Five years of development could not reveal every consequence of releasing it into a world already shaped by another invention. The waste-eaters changed. Beneath shells of discarded metal, the life we had entrusted with our future became a threat to it.

By the time humanity understood the price of delay, almost everyone was gone. The survivors gathered in orbit and called their refuge H.O.P.E. You are one of its cadets. Reclaim what can still be used. Isolate what keeps the danger spreading. Give the people who remain a chance to build something they will finally take responsibility for.

## 5. Show the theme through action

- Before the disaster, show cleaned sites filling with new waste while transport continues. A working cleanup creature beside an expanding waste stream communicates the contradiction without a lecture.
- Distinguish the two programs visually: biological processing already in use, then a later atmospheric-treatment deployment. The audience must not mistake the chemical for the invention that originally created life.
- After the disaster, repeat familiar waste shapes as shells around moving organisms. The threat visibly carries the evidence of earlier neglect.
- At HOPE, show patched equipment, careful material recovery and maintenance. Paid repair reflects scarce resources; it is not a moral punishment for being poor or injured.
- In the ending, show containment and cleanup continuing after the boss is gone. The survivors have begun work that cannot be postponed again.

No morality meter, blame score, dialogue-choice system, additional currency or gameplay penalty is introduced. This is narrative and presentation guidance only.
